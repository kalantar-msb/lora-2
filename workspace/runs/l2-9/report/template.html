Residency-aware LoRA routing cuts TTFT tails up to 4× under adapter-slot pressure

The lora-residency-scorer beat the stock lora-affinity-scorer in all 12 conditions tested: lower TTFT p90 and p99, lower mean E2E, and lower TTFT p99 for nearly every adapter. Median TTFT, decode speed (TPOT) and base-model requests were unaffected.

Servers
granite-3.3-2b-instruct on 4 vLLM pods, each with 3 GPU and 8 CPU adapter slots
Workload
27 LoRA adapters, Zipf popularity (α = 1.5 unless noted), Poisson arrivals, 256-token prompts, 32-token mean outputs unless noted
Arms
Same profile (queue 2, KV cache 2, prefix 3) plus a LoRA scorer at weight 6: lora-affinity or lora-residency
Runs
One run per condition; cells last 40 min at 7.5 req/s down to about 1 min at 45 req/s

  • Load

    The advantage grows with load. Mean E2E is 10% lower at 22.5 req/s, 44% at 30 and 59% at 45. 22.5 req/s, just past the knee, is the weakest point.

  • Output length

    Longer outputs hold adapter slots longer, so they act like more load: 7.5 req/s at 128 tokens overloads more than 30 req/s at 32 tokens. The gain grows to a 58% lower mean E2E at 15 req/s.

  • Popularity

    Flatter popularity (α = 1.0) runs out of slots already at 15 req/s and gives the largest gain: TTFT p90 3.7× lower and mean E2E 63% lower at 30 req/s.

  • Base model

    20% base-model traffic keeps a 130 ms median TTFT in both arms, a shorter tail under loraresidency, and leaves the adapter gains intact.

  • Cost

    TPOT is within a few ms between arms everywhere. Under overload, TTFT dominates E2E even with long outputs.

Why it works, and what it does not hurt

The bottleneck is adapter slots; loraresidency keeps adapters where their traffic goes

Memory and compute were never the limit: KV cache stayed at or below 1.3% with no preemptions. vLLM runs at most 3 distinct adapters per batch on a pod and skips requests for any other, so requests wait whenever their adapter has no slot. The residency scorer prices those waits and the evictions a request would cause, and routes to avoid them.

vLLM never holds a base-model request back for a slot, so their median stays near 130 ms. Baseline's base-model tail is longer, probably because its more frequent adapter loads briefly stall each pod (not verified). Where loraresidency concentrated adapter traffic, the queue scorer sent base-model requests to quieter pods: the pod with 52% of adapter requests got 2.5% of base-model requests.

Limits of this evidence

  • One run per condition. The 15 and 30 req/s points at α = 1.5 were repeated in run l2-8 with matching results.
  • High-load cells are short: 2.5 min at 30 req/s and about 1 min at 45.
  • One small model, 4 pods with 3 GPU slots each, and a synthetic workload made of copies of 9 adapters.
  • Scorer weights fixed at 6 against 6. Weight sensitivity and larger slot counts are not yet tested.
  • At moderate load with steep popularity, loraresidency put up to 65% of requests on one pod. It cost nothing here, but these pods were far from compute limits.
  • Under the heaviest overload, loraresidency spread the top adapter over more pods and slowed it: TTFT p90 0.1 → 1.4 s at 15 req/s with 128 tokens.
